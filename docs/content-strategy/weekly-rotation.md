# The Weekly Rotation

Instapdown's 2026 recommendation, adapted. Four posts a week, four different formulas, so
the feed never reads as formulaic and you learn which formula your audience actually
rewards.

## The week

| Day | Post | Formula | Length | Filmed |
|-----|------|---------|--------|--------|
| **Monday** | POV Reel | 2 | Under 15 sec | Sunday |
| **Wednesday** | Specific Outcome Reel | 1 or 7 | Under 30 sec | Sunday |
| **Friday** | Contrarian Reel | 3 | Under 30 sec | Sunday |
| **Sunday** | Facebook long-form video | 8 cold open | About 3 min | The field trip itself |

**Monday, POV.** "POV: your mom is a Boeing toolmaker and today's field trip is..." Under
15 seconds. Let Noah deliver it.

**Wednesday, Specific Outcome.** Numbers heavy. "We visited 3 airports in one day and
here's what my boys remember." The number is the hook, not the footage.

**Friday, Contrarian.** "Museum tours are the worst way to teach kids about aviation.
Here's what we do instead." This is the post that gets shared and argued with. That is
the job.

**Sunday, the signature.** The three-minute adventure recap with a story arc, kid
narration, one lesson per video, and a warm outro. This is where the Bucket List Family
cinematic style lives, and where the beautiful footage finally earns its place.

## The Sunday planning session

Thirty minutes, same order every week.

1. **Look at the week's field trip** from the aviation-homeschool calendar. That is the
   location. Do not invent a trip to fit a hook.
2. **Pick 4 hooks from `swipe-file.md`** before you decide anything else. One POV, one
   outcome, one contrarian, one confessional or cold open.
3. **Write them at the top of the planning sheet.** The hooks come first. The shot list
   is built to serve the hooks, not the other way around.
4. **Check the "still needs" column** on each. Anything unfilled, fill it or swap the hook.
5. **Assign the kid-fronted one.** At least one of the four is delivered by Ethan or Noah.
6. **Pick the download** each post points to on runwaysnpoppies.com.
7. **Mark the formulas used** so next week draws from different rows of the rotation index.

## The call to action

Every post ends pointing somewhere. For Runways n Poppies that is the free library, not a
vague "follow along."

- "The lab sheet we used is free at runwaysnpoppies.com."
- "Comment PLANE and I'll send you the trip card for this one."
- "The whole 52-week plan is on the site. No email required."

Never say "link in bio." Never say "come learn with us." Both are on the do-not-use list
in `hook-formulas.md`.

## Reading the results

Every two to three weeks, open Facebook Insights and Instagram Insights and answer one
question: **which formula held attention longest for your audience?** Not which post got
the most likes. Which one held.

Double down on the winner for a few weeks. Keep the rotation alive underneath so you do
not narrow to a single trick.

## Three moves to start

1. **Pick 4 hooks** from the swipe file, one of each formula, and write them at the top of
   this Sunday's planning sheet before choosing the location.
2. **Run the ten-minute study drill** in `creator-study.md`. Nine Reels, first two seconds
   only, exact opening lines written down.
3. **Record one Reel where Noah or Ethan delivers the hook, not you.** Kid-fronted hooks
   outperform parent-fronted ones for family-adventure audiences. Let them own the first
   two seconds.
