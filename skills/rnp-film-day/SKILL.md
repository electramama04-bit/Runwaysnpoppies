# Runways n Poppies — Film Day

One trip has to feed four posts. Monday POV, Wednesday outcome, Friday contrarian, Sunday
long-form. If you come home with only wide shots of airplanes, you have B-roll and no
posts, because B-roll is seconds 3 to 15 and never the hook.

## The rule the whole skill hangs on

**Film the hook first, before anything else.** Before the tickets, before the first
exhibit, before anybody is hungry. The hook is 8 to 12 words and takes ninety seconds to
capture. It is also the single thing you cannot fake later, because a tired kid at hour
four does not sound like a kid who just arrived somewhere exciting.

Everything else on the trip can be reshot from memory. The hook cannot.

## Before you leave the house — five minutes

- [ ] The four hooks are on the planning sheet, from **rnp-reel-hooks**. You are filming
      to a line, not filming and hoping a line appears.
- [ ] Who says each one. At least one is Ethan or Noah.
- [ ] Phone at 100%, and a battery pack in the bag. Video eats a phone.
- [ ] At least 8 GB free. Check it. A full phone at the cockpit door is a lost post.
- [ ] Lock the phone to vertical. Reels and Facebook video are 9:16.
- [ ] `references/shot-card.md` screenshotted to your phone. It is the whole plan on one
      screen, for when you are standing somewhere loud and cannot read a skill file.
- [ ] Read `references/location-notes.md` for this location. If it is not in there yet,
      today you find out and write it down.

## The capture list — one trip, four posts

| Shot | Feeds | Length | When on the trip |
|------|-------|--------|------------------|
| **1. The hook, 3 takes** | Monday POV | 10 sec each | First, in the parking lot |
| **2. The arrival reaction** | Sunday cold open | 15 sec | Walking in, camera on faces |
| **3. The moment** | Sunday payoff | 30 to 60 sec | Whenever it happens, unplanned |
| **4. The number shot** | Wednesday outcome | 20 sec | Whatever proves the count |
| **5. The kid explaining** | Friday contrarian | 30 sec | After they have learned the thing |
| **6. B-roll, six clips** | All four, seconds 3 to 15 | 5 to 8 sec each | Last, while they are tired |

Six items. If you get all six, you have a full week. If the day falls apart, shots 1 and 3
are the two that must survive.

### 1. The hook, three takes

Same line three times. Do not coach between takes beyond "again, a little faster." The
third take is almost always the one. Film in the car or against a wall out of the wind.

### 2. The arrival reaction

Camera on their faces, not on the building. Walk in behind them and shoot forward over
their shoulders. The viewer wants the kid's face at the moment the thing gets big, and no
sign or entrance arch has ever done that job.

### 3. The moment

This is the whole Sunday video and it is never on the schedule. It is Noah going quiet in
front of something, or Ethan correcting an adult, or a question neither of you can answer.
**When it starts, film. Do not wait to see if it becomes something.** You can delete
thirty seconds of nothing. You cannot recover the one you watched happen.

If you miss it, do not restage it. Tell it to camera instead, in the car, as a
confessional. An honest retelling beats a fake reenactment every time.

### 4. The number shot

Wednesday's post is built on a number, so film the proof. Three airport signs. Two diner
receipts. The odometer. A count of something on camera. A number you can see beats a
number you claim.

### 5. The kid explaining

Point the camera at the kid and ask one question: "So how does that actually work?" Let
them answer wrong. A 9-year-old getting it 80 percent right is more watchable than a
parent getting it 100 percent right, and the comments will finish the other 20 percent.

### 6. B-roll, last

Six clips, five to eight seconds each, held still. Wings, tools, hands, a taxiing plane, a
propeller, the walk back to the car. Shoot it at the end when everybody is worn out,
because B-roll needs nothing from them.

**B-roll is never the first shot of a post.** That is the first rule in **rnp-reel-hooks**
and it is the most common way a good trip turns into a post nobody watches.

## Overnight and multi-day trips

Five trips this year run two days or more: Spokane MAC (Sep 19-20), San Francisco
(Oct 10-12), and Dayton (Mar 8-12), plus any overnight that gets added.

A two-day trip is **two weeks of posts, not one**. Treat each day as its own capture list.

- **Film a fresh hook on the morning of day 2.** Not a leftover from day 1. Day 2 has its
  own arrival, its own reaction, its own moment.
- Day 1 gets the road trip angle. Day 2 gets the payoff angle. That is two different
  Sunday videos, not one long one.
- On a multi-venue day, ask the filming questions at **every desk**. Four venues in San
  Francisco means four policies.
- Dump both days separately, same night each. `2026-09-19-spokane-mac` and
  `2026-09-20-mobius`. Merging them is how footage gets lost.

## Audio — the thing that actually ruins airfield footage

Wind is the enemy at every location on your calendar. Paine Field, any tarmac, any ramp,
any observation deck.

- Film every spoken line **in the car, in a hangar, or in the lee of a building.** Never in
  open air on a ramp.
- Get the phone within about two feet of the speaker's mouth. Phone mics fall off fast.
- Record ten seconds of nothing before each take. It gives the edit room to breathe.
- If a jet is running, do not fight it. Wait, or make the noise the point and caption it.

Bad audio kills more homeschool videos than bad video does. Viewers forgive a shaky frame.
They do not forgive not being able to hear a kid.

## Directing a 9-year-old without making him stiff

- **Never say "say it like this."** Say what it means and let him pick the words. The line
  can drift. The formula is what matters, not the wording.
- Give him the job, not the script. "Tell the camera where we are and who your mom is."
- Film him before he knows you started. The first natural take beats the fourth performed one.
- Three takes, then stop. Past three he is acting, and acting reads as fake to exactly the
  audience you want.
- Ethan at 14 can carry a full mission promise and a sixty-second explanation. Noah at 9
  carries a POV line or a cold open. Do not hand a 9-year-old a paragraph.

## Consent, every time

Ask both boys before anything with their face in it goes public. Not once, per trip.

If either says no, that trip is Mama-fronted and B-roll, and the post still works. A kid
who knows he can say no is a kid who says yes naturally on camera, and that is the whole
reason kid-fronted hooks hold attention in the first place.

Josh sees anything before it posts if a kid's face is in it.

## Where you may not be able to film

**Never assume. Confirm on site, then write it into `references/location-notes.md`.**

- **Boeing facilities.** Mama works there. Employee status makes filming more restricted,
  not less. Nothing from inside a Boeing facility goes on camera without explicit clearance
  from Boeing, and "nobody stopped me" is not clearance. When in doubt, film outside the
  fence and talk about the work instead of showing it.
- **Factory and Future of Flight tours.** Photo and video rules vary by tour and change.
  Ask at the desk before the tour starts, not during.
- **Any ramp, tarmac, or movement area.** Access is escorted and permission is
  case-by-case. Ask the FBO or the operator first.
- **Museums.** Handheld phone video is usually fine, tripods and gimbals often are not.
  Ask about the tripod before you set it up.

## The sixty-second dump, same day

Before bed, not next Saturday.

1. Move the day's clips into one folder named `YYYY-MM-DD-location`.
2. Watch shot 1 and shot 3 only. Confirm they are usable.
3. Write one line of what actually happened, in your own words, while you still remember it.
   That line is Sunday's caption opening.
4. Add anything new to `references/location-notes.md` — wind, noise, access, what was worth
   filming, what was not.

If shots 1 and 3 did not survive, say so now. A reshoot next week is possible. A reshoot
five weeks later is not.

## What NOT to do

- Don't film the hook last. It is the one thing the trip cannot replace.
- Don't restage the moment. Retell it to camera instead.
- Don't film spoken lines in open wind.
- Don't hand a 9-year-old a paragraph.
- Don't post a kid's face without asking that day.
- Don't come home with only B-roll and expect a week of posts out of it.
