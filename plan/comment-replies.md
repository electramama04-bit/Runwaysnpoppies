# #MakeLifeFun — Comment Reply Bank
**Save this to your phone Notes app before you post. Not after.**

## The six rules, before any reply

1. **Answer once. Never twice.** One reply per person, ever. If they come back swinging,
   you heart it and walk away. A second reply is where you lose.
2. **The comment section is a stage, not a conversation.** You are never writing to the
   person who attacked you. You are writing to the four hundred people reading over their
   shoulder who haven't decided about you yet. Write to them.
3. **Never name the teacher, the school, the principal, or the district.** Not in a post,
   not in a reply, not in a DM, not "just to a friend." Ever. This protects Noah, it
   protects you, and it is the single line that keeps this a story about a boy instead of
   a fight with an employer.
4. **Concede the true part first.** Every hostile comment has one true thing in it. Say
   that part out loud before you say anything else. It disarms the reader, and it's honest.
5. **Delete, don't debate:** threats, anything about Noah's body or appearance, anyone
   trying to identify his school, and anyone in your DMs asking where you live. Block, no
   reply, no screenshot, no callout post.
6. **Do not open comments after 9 p.m.** You are a toolmaker who was up at 4. Tired-you
   writes replies that morning-you has to live with. Check them in the morning.

## REPLY 1 — "You're exploiting your kid for views."

> "You're using your son's trauma for clicks. He's going to hate you for this."

**Your reply:**

> That's a fair thing to worry about and I've worried about it too. Here's how we handle
> it: Noah watches every video before it goes up and he can kill any of them, no reason
> required. He's killed one already. His face only shows up in the happy half. We never
> re-enact anything that happened to him. If he ever tells me he's done, I'm done — the
> channel isn't worth more to me than he is. Thanks for keeping an eye out for him.

**Why it works:** you don't defend, you show your process. "He's killed one already" is
the sentence that ends the argument, because nobody can argue with a system that's already
running. *(Only say it once it's true — let him kill one.)*

## REPLY 2 — "Sounds like your kid really is the problem."

> "Maybe he actually needed that structure. Ever consider you're the problem and he's
> just a spoiled kid who can't sit still?"

**Your reply:**

> I've considered it more than you'd think — that's what those meetings do to you, you go
> home at night and ask yourself if you're the one who's wrong. Here's where I landed: a
> plan built before anybody met him wasn't a response to him. It was a response to a
> file. I'm open to being wrong about a lot, but not about that one.

**Why it works:** you refuse to get defensive, you show you actually sat with the doubt,
and then you hold one narrow line instead of defending everything. Readers trust the
person who concedes ground.

> "Great, another homeschool kid who'll have no social skills and can't function in the

## REPLY 4 — "School shootings are real. Schools HAVE to take this seriously."
### ⚠️ THIS IS THE ONE THAT DECIDES WHETHER THE FINGER-GUN POST SURVIVES. Get it right.

> "My cousin's school had a shooting. Finger-guns are how it starts. You should be
> grateful they took it seriously instead of making a video about it."

**Your reply:**

> I'm not going to argue with you about this one, and I'm sorry about your cousin's
> school — that's not a thing I'd wave off. You're right that schools have to take
> threats seriously. My argument isn't that they should stop caring. It's that a
> checklist can't tell the difference between a boy playing and a boy in trouble, and
> a person who actually knows the kid can. We replaced judgment with a process, and now
> the process catches nine-year-olds saying "pew" while the kids who genuinely need help
> get the same form filled out. I want them to be better at this, not to care less.

**Why it works:** you lead with the loss, not with your point. You give away the entire
safety argument, then narrow to a claim they can actually agree with — *the process is bad
at its job.* Most of the people who came in hot will land here with you.

**Never say in this thread:** "back in my day," "snowflake," anything about politics,
anything sarcastic. Not one word. This is the thread where warmth is your only weapon.

Blue Tape reel will do its job whether or not you're in the comments, and it will still
be doing it in the morning when you're rested.
