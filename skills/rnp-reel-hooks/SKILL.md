# Runways n Poppies — Reel Hooks

The hook is the first 1.5 seconds. It decides whether a thumb stops. Beautiful footage of
an airplane does not stop a thumb. A number, a challenge, or an unexpected reveal does.

Save the beautiful footage for seconds 3 to 15, after the viewer has already committed.

**Fun first, lesson second, always.** The aerospace credibility shows up at seconds 5 to
15. Never at second 0. The moment a hook sounds like curriculum, the scroll wins.

## Family facts — never get these wrong on camera

| Fact | Truth |
|------|-------|
| The kids | Ethan (14) and Noah (9). Two sons. **There is no daughter.** |
| Ages | Noah is 9 through the 2026-2027 school year. Do not write "my 10-year-old." |
| Boeing tenure | **Unconfirmed.** Ask before any hook says "[N] years at Boeing." |
| Brand name | Always "Runways n Poppies" with spaces. Never RnP on camera. |
| Retired name | There was an earlier pre-launch working name. It is retired. Never use it, in a hook, a caption, or a hashtag. |

Source research for this skill used "daughter" and "my 10-year-old" throughout. Those were
wrong. A hook that names a kid who does not exist is the kind of thing a viewer notices
once and never trusts again.

## When to activate

- "Give me a hook for the [location] reel"
- "What do I open Monday's post with?"
- "This opening is weak, fix it"
- "What should Noah say first?"
- Whenever **rnp-content-calendar** needs four hooks for the Sunday planning sheet.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Get the trip and the slot

Two things before picking anything:

1. **The trip.** Which field trip, which day, from the aviation-homeschool calendar. Do
   not invent a trip to fit a hook. If the trip is missing, ask.
2. **The slot.** Monday POV, Wednesday outcome, Friday contrarian, or Sunday long-form.
   That decides the formula. If there is no slot, decide from length using the table below.

### Step 2 — Pick the formula

| # | Formula | Retention | Use when |
|---|---------|----------:|----------|
| 1 | Specific Outcome (numbers) | 45% | Any Reel under 30 sec |
| 2 | POV | 42% | Less-saturated niches, which is yours |
| 3 | Contrarian | 38% | Saying something the homeschool world disagrees with |
| 4 | Curiosity Gap (tease and reveal) | Strong 3-sec hold | 15 to 30 sec |
| 5 | Direct Address + Stake | High | 30+ sec |
| 6 | Confessional | High | Talking to camera, usually in the car |
| 7 | Number That Demands Explanation | Very high | Under 15 sec |
| 8 | Specific-Scene Cold Open | Very high | 30+ sec, and every Sunday long-form |

Retention figures are OpusClip's analysis of 1.9 million short-form clips, via Instapdown
and CreatorHouse, 2026.

Then read `references/swipe-file.md` and pull from that formula's row in the rotation
index. Never pull the same formula two posts running.

### Step 3 — Fill every "still needs"

Each hook in the swipe file carries a **Still needs** column naming the one fact that has
to be true before it can be spoken. Fill it with something real from the actual trip.

A generic fill kills the hook. "A few airports" gets scrolled past. "3 airports in 4 hours"
does not. If the fact is not known, ask Mama. Do not invent a number, a museum, or a
coworker quote.

### Step 4 — Run the first-3-words test

Read the first three words alone. Do they create curiosity, surprise, or pull?

- "Here's what I" fails. No pull.
- "We flew 3" passes. Number plus open loop.
- "Museum tours are" passes. Sets up a fight.

Cut filler off the front. The strongest word goes first.

### Step 5 — Check for kid-fronted

**If a kid can deliver the line, the kid delivers the line.** A for Adley and the Norris
Nuts both prove kid-fronted hooks hold longer than parent-fronted ones for family-adventure
audiences. Ethan at 14 can carry a mission promise. Noah at 9 can carry a POV or a cold
open. At least one of every four hooks is theirs.

### Step 6 — Return three, recommend one

Return the strongest hook, name its formula, and offer two alternates from different
formulas. If called by rnp-content-calendar, hand back all four slots at once.

## When the swipe file doesn't have it

The 40 hooks cover the locations and angles known as of September 2026. A new trip, a new
season, or a new angle will not be in there. When that happens:

1. Pick the formula first, same as Step 2.
2. Read `references/hook-library.md` in the **viral-hooks** skill and pull a template from
   the matching category.
3. Fill it with the real specifics from the trip.
4. Run Steps 4 and 5 on it exactly the same way.
5. **Add the finished hook to `references/swipe-file.md`** with its number, formula, and
   a "still needs" note. The swipe file is supposed to grow. A hook that worked once and
   was never written down is a hook that gets reinvented badly six months later.

## Hook length

| Slot | Hook length | Whole video |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Monday POV | 8 words or fewer | Under 15 sec |
| Wednesday outcome | 12 words or fewer | Under 30 sec |
| Friday contrarian | 12 words or fewer | Under 30 sec |
| Sunday long-form | 12 words or fewer, spoken over the cold open | About 3 min |

Twelve words is the hard ceiling for the caption's line 1. If it runs longer, one idea is
hiding in there with a second idea. Cut the weaker one.

## Output shape

Return it ready to film and ready to paste into the caption's first line:

```
RECOMMENDED — Formula [N], [name]
"[the hook]"
Spoken by: [Mama / Ethan / Noah]
First-3-words test: [the three words] — pass, [why]
Still needs: [anything Mama has to confirm, or "nothing, ready to film"]

ALTERNATES
2. Formula [N] — "[hook]"
3. Formula [N] — "[hook]"
```

## The four things that kill a hook

1. **Leading with pretty sky or plane footage.** Scenery loses to conflict, surprise, and
   numbers. B-roll belongs at seconds 3 to 15.
2. **Saying "come learn with us" or "let's explore."** These are the exact phrases the
   algorithm skips. They read as classroom marketing. Say what happened *today* instead.
3. **Vague numbers.** Specificity is the whole game.
4. **Front-loading the curriculum.** Fun first, lesson second.

## Caption rules that carry over

The hook is line 1 of the caption. The body follows Mama's standing voice rules from
**vfw-969-social-captions**, which apply to every brand she runs:

- **No em-dashes.** Periods or commas. This is the most important rule.
- No emojis in the caption body. Hashtags go in their own field.
- Short sentences, about a 6th grade reading level, first person, present tense.
- Forbidden: delve, unleash, game-changer, unlock, dive in, "link in bio."
- Every post ends pointing at the free library on runwaysnpoppies.com, never a vague
  "follow along."

## What NOT to do

- Don't return a hook with a bracket or a "still needs" left unfilled.
- Don't stack two hooks. One hook, one idea.
- Don't reuse a formula two posts in a row. Rotate through the index.
- Don't put the aviation lesson in the hook. That is the whole thesis of this skill.
