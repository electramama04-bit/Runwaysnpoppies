# Creator Study — Who to Watch and What to Take

The homeschool creators (Pike, Bogart, Waldock) sell curriculum, so their hooks are
teaching-flavored. That is exactly the wrong model for Facebook and Reels. Study the
families who make adventure with kids look fun and let the learning land as a bonus.

## The core three

### 1. The Bucket List Family — @thebucketlistfamily

- **Reach:** 3M Instagram, 1M+ YouTube
- **Who:** Garrett and Jessica Gee sold their house in 2015 and have traveled full time
  with their three kids ever since. 90+ countries.
- **Format:** Warm, cinematic YouTube long-form, 10 to 20 minutes, paired with punchy
  Reels. Cold opens drop you inside a moment before the destination is ever named.
- **Steal:** The specific-scene cold open. Start in the middle, not with a location card.
- **Your version:** "We're 41,000 feet up and the pilot just handed my kid the checklist."

### 2. The Ohana Adventure — @theohanaadventure

- **Reach:** 414K Instagram, 3M+ YouTube
- **Who:** Utah family of nine. Road trips, RV life, homeschooling on the move, and
  stunts like buying a private island.
- **Why they matter most to you:** They already combine homeschool, adventure, and big
  family logistics. Closest lifestyle match in the set. Fun first, education implied.
- **Steal:** The bold-claim reveal. The whole video is the payoff of one
  impossible-sounding sentence.
- **Your version:** "We took the boys to the Museum of Flight and let them pick tonight's
  dinner based on what they built."

### 3. Outdoor Boys — Luke Nichols

- **Reach:** 3M+ YouTube
- **Who:** Luke takes his three boys camping, fishing, foraging, and flying into remote
  spots on float planes.
- **Why he matters:** Proof that parent plus kids plus a real mission beats polished
  production every time. No costumes, no script, zero teacher voice.
- **Steal:** The mission promise. The title is the hook.
- **Your version:** "We drove to Boeing's Everett factory and the boys figured out how a
  wing is built before the tour guide finished his sentence."

## The ten-minute study drill

Do this once, then again every month or so.

1. Open three Reels from The Bucket List Family, three from The Ohana Adventure, three
   from Outdoor Boys. Nine total.
2. Watch **only the first two seconds** of each. Stop the video.
3. Write down the exact opening line, word for word. Nine lines.
4. Mark which of the eight formulas in `hook-formulas.md` each one used.

You are not studying the destination. You are studying the first breath.

## Kid-fronted is the single biggest lever

A for Adley and the Norris Nuts both prove that when the child delivers the first two
seconds, family-adventure audiences hold longer than when the parent does. Ethan at 14
can carry a full mission promise. Noah at 9 can carry a cold open or a POV line. Let
them say it. Your voice belongs in seconds 5 to 15, where the aerospace credibility
lands.
