# Runways n Poppies — Comments

**Open this before a heavy post goes live, not after.** Read runways-n-poppies-voice first —
every guardrail there applies in the comments too, especially never naming the school.

---

## The six rules

1. **Answer once. Never twice.** One reply per person, ever. If they come back swinging, heart
   it and walk away. The second reply is where she loses.
2. **The comment section is a stage, not a conversation.** She is never writing to the person
   who attacked her. She is writing to the four hundred people reading over their shoulder who
   haven't decided about her yet.
3. **Never name the teacher, the school, or the district.** Not even when someone demands it.
   Especially not then.
4. **Concede the true part first.** Every hostile comment contains one true thing. Say that out
   loud before saying anything else. It disarms the reader and it's honest.
5. **Delete, don't debate:** threats, anything about Noah's body or appearance, anyone trying
   to identify his school, anyone asking where they live. Block, no reply, no screenshot, no
   callout post.
6. **Do not open comments after 9 p.m.** She's a toolmaker who was up at 4. Tired-her writes
   replies that morning-her has to live with.

## The reply shape

Every good reply is the same four moves, in this order:

1. **Give away the true part** — completely, without qualifying it.
2. **Show the process, don't defend the choice** — "here's how we handle it" beats "you're wrong."
3. **Narrow to one claim** she'll actually hold. Never defend everything; defend one thing.
4. **Stop.** No sign-off, no invitation to continue.

## The five types

**1 — "You're exploiting your kid for views."**
Concede it's a fair worry. Then show the system: he previews everything, he can kill any post
with no reason given, his face is in the happy half only, nothing is ever re-enacted. The
sentence that ends the argument is a real one he already vetoed — nobody can argue with a
system that's already running. Only say it once it's true.

**2 — "Sounds like your kid really is the problem."**
Admit she's asked herself that, because she has. Then hold one narrow line: a plan built before
anyone met him was a response to a file, not to him. Readers trust the person who concedes ground.

**3 — "Homeschooled kids are unsocialized."**
Don't argue the abstract claim. Answer with one week of specifics — every adult he actually
talked to, and how few he was permitted to talk to before. Specifics beat theory.

**4 — "School shootings are real. Schools HAVE to take this seriously."**
⚠️ **This is the one that decides whether the finger-gun post survives.**
Lead with the loss, not the point. Give away the entire safety argument — schools do have to
take threats seriously, and the fear is legitimate. Then narrow to the claim most of them will
actually agree with: **a checklist can't tell a boy playing from a boy in trouble, and a person
who knows the kid can.** She wants them better at this, not caring less.
**Never in this thread:** "back in my day," "snowflake," politics, or sarcasm. Not one word.
Warmth is the only weapon that works here.

**5 — "You're trashing teachers."**
Agree with all of it. That teacher had thirty kids and no help and reached for the tool she had.
Relocate the blame to the system that handed one adult an impossible job. This reply converts
teachers into her audience — several will share it.

## The harder situations

**Someone from the school comments.** Do not reply publicly. Do not delete it either — deleting
reads as guilt. Screenshot it, hide it if it names anyone, and walk away. Nothing typed here helps.

**A parent in her DMs who is in it right now.** Lead with "your kid is not broken, and you're
not crazy for feeling how you feel." Say plainly she's not a counselor and won't pretend to be.
Then tell her what *they* did and what it cost — and **never tell her to pull her kid out.**
That's her marriage, her budget, her state, her call.

**Somebody makes it partisan.** One line — this isn't left or right, it's a nine-year-old thing,
and she's heard from mothers of every stripe with the same story. Then stop replying in that
thread entirely. Partisan threads are where good posts go to die.

**Somebody asks about transcripts, college, or the law.** Real question, answer it honestly, and
offer the paperwork post. It's the highest-trust, lowest-emotion content she can make, and it's
the part nobody shows.

## When to close the app

If her chest goes tight, close it. The post does not need defending. It will still be doing its
job in the morning when she's rested.

**One honest tear on camera, once. Zero tears in the comment section.**
